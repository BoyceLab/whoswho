How to use this

Type a gene symbol above. The gene's card opens, with three views you switch between using the tabs under the gene name: the same facts, arranged for what each reader needs.

  • Patients and caregivers get the disorder name, the organizations serving families with that gene, registries and natural history studies to join, the platforms it is enrolled in, treatments in development, and any FDA patient meeting.
  • Advocacy groups get a checklist of what is on record and what is missing, plus a link to correct it.
  • Clinicians and researchers get the evidence label from every source that includes the gene, the mechanism class, and links to ClinVar, gnomAD, GeneReviews, PubMed, and ClinicalTrials.gov.

Use all genes to compare many at once, filter by tier, domain, mechanism, or source, and download a subset.

Search takes a gene symbol or HGNC ID. Older symbols resolve to the current one, so a report that says SCN2A1 finds SCN2A. Each card has its own address, such as gene.html?symbol=KCNT1, so it can be linked from anywhere.

What the labels mean

T1 the strongest expert reviews agree this gene causes the condition. T2 good evidence, with less agreement across sources. T3 on at least one list, with limited or disputed evidence.

Verified next to an organization means a reviewer confirmed it. Awaiting review means it came from a public roster and has not been checked yet.

Not on record for a registry means whoswho has no entry, not that the registry excludes the gene. Registries add conditions often, so the card links you to check directly.

Where this comes from

Clinical gene panels, curated research gene lists, registry platforms, coalition rosters, and advocacy directories, joined on a single gene identifier so that agreement and disagreement between them are visible per gene rather than hidden inside one merged label.

Every release records the version or snapshot date and checksum of each public source, so a number can be traced and reproduced. The full list, and the rules used to assign tiers and mechanism classes, are on the all genes page.

Corrections and additions

Anyone running a foundation, family group, Facebook community, or registry can update their own listing. No account needed; the form opens an email.

Anything else, including corrections about a gene rather than an organization, goes to danielle@boycedatascience.com.
