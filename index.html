whoswho

Type a gene and find the patient organizations, registries, and research resources that exist for it, alongside how strongly each evidence source ties the gene to epilepsy or a neurodevelopmental disorder. One list, built from the clinical panels, curated gene lists, registry platforms, and advocacy directories that otherwise have to be searched one at a time.

Patients and caregivers

You have a gene name from a genetic test report and want to know who else is working on it. Look the gene up and open its row. You will see the disorder name, the organizations that serve families with that gene, the registries and natural history studies you can join, the research platforms it is enrolled in (such as Simons Searchlight), and any FDA patient listening session or patient-focused drug development meeting held for it.

  • A T1 label means the strongest sources agree the gene causes the condition; T2 means good evidence with less agreement; T3 means the gene is on at least one list but the evidence is limited or disputed.
  • "Candidate" next to an organization means it came from a public roster and has not yet been checked by a reviewer.
  • Nothing here is medical advice. Take what you find to your care team and the organization itself.

Patient advocacy groups

See how your gene and your organization are recorded, and where the gaps are. The review queue lists genes with strong evidence and no organization on record, T1 first, which is where a new group or a coalition can make the most difference. Candidate rows are the ones waiting for confirmation; a confirmed row records registry platform, natural history study, IRB status, coalitions, and maturity stage.

  • Filter organizations by status to see what is confirmed and what is still a candidate.
  • Download the filtered table as CSV to review offline.
  • Update your own listing: correct what is here, add your registry, or record your maturity stage. You do not need a GitHub account.
  • Self-assess against the research readiness maturity model: five stages from an informal community to sharing standardized data, across five tracks.
  • Corrections and additions: email danielle@boycedatascience.com.

Clinicians and researchers

Compare the lists. Every gene row shows which sources include it and the evidence label each source assigned (ClinGen classification, PanelApp color, Genes4Epilepsy inclusion, and so on), so agreement and disagreement are visible per gene rather than hidden inside one merged label. Genes are keyed on HGNC ID, with ORPHAcodes carried for the disorder join, and the mechanism class (channelopathy, synaptopathy, mTORopathy, chromatinopathy) is a filter.

  • Every release records the version or snapshot date and checksum of each source, so a number can be reproduced.
  • The tier and domain rules are stated in full on the Method section and in the code.
  • Download the whole spine or any filtered subset as CSV; JSON is in the repository.

How to use this

  1. Type a gene symbol in the box above and press Look it up. The gene's card opens.
  2. The card has three views; switch between Patients and caregivers, Advocacy groups, and Clinicians and researchers with the tabs under the gene name. The same gene, the same facts, arranged for what each reader needs.
  3. Patients and caregivers see the disorder, organizations, registries and studies to join, treatments in development, and a plain-language statement of how strong the evidence is.
  4. Advocacy groups see a checklist of what is on record for the gene and what is missing.
  5. Clinicians and researchers see the evidence label from every source that includes the gene, the mechanism class, and links to ClinVar, gnomAD, GeneReviews, PubMed, and ClinicalTrials.gov.
  6. Explore the full table when you want to compare many genes at once, filter by tier, domain, mechanism, or source, or download a subset.

Search takes a gene symbol or HGNC ID. Older symbols resolve to the current one during the build, so a report that says SCN2A1 finds SCN2A. Each card has its own address, for example gene.html?symbol=KCNT1, so it can be linked from anywhere.
