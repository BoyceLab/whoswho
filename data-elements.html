What registry platforms ask

Choosing a registry platform is partly a question of what it will let you collect. This compares the instruments and domains in platforms used by rare epilepsy and neurodevelopmental organizations, drawn from published data dictionaries and survey supplements.

Two platforms collecting the same number of variables are not equivalent. What matters for combining data later is whether variables are coded to a standard, which the last column shows.

Reading this

Disease scope says whether the platform's question set is written for one condition or for any condition. Disease-agnostic sets are easier to combine across genes; disease-specific sets usually capture more of what matters to one community.

Coded to a standard counts variables mapped to Human Phenotype Ontology terms. Coded variables can be pooled with other datasets without a mapping exercise first; uncoded ones need that work before they can be combined.

The maturity model calls the step where this matters harmonized to standards, on the data infrastructure track.
