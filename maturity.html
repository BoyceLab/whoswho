Research readiness maturity model

Most landscapes describe what exists. This one also describes a path. Every rare disease organization moves, at its own pace, from an informal community toward sharing standardized data that outlives any single group. The model names the stages on that path so an organization can see where it stands, where it is ahead, and where it is behind.

It is populated by self-assessment, not assigned from outside, and it is descriptive rather than a ranking.

The five stages

An organization sits in one of five overall stages based on where it stands across the tracks below.

EmergingAn informal community exists. No legal or research infrastructure.
OrganizedIncorporated and governed. Beginning to think about research.
ConnectedPlugged into the broader research ecosystem. Collecting structured information about patients.
Data GeneratingOperating registries or natural history infrastructure. Producing data with intent.
Data SharingContributing standardized, documented data or metadata to shared or open resources for use beyond the organization.
How the composite level works. An organization's composite level is roughly the lowest stage it has reliably reached across the core tracks. You cannot be Data Sharing overall if you have no governance. The tracks show exactly where an organization is ahead or behind.

The five tracks

Each track runs through the same five stages. Reading across a track shows what good looks like one step ahead, and the kind of support that tends to help at each step.

Organizational foundation

StageQuestions to ask yourselvesWhat tends to help
Informal communityDo we have a space where patients and families find each other? Does someone informally keep things going?Plain language orientation on nonprofit formation; introductions to founders who have made the leap.
Unincorporated associationDo we have identified leaders, even without legal status? Is our mission written down?Pro bono legal help for incorporation and bylaws; governance templates; board recruitment guidance.
Incorporated nonprofitAre we a registered nonprofit with bylaws and a functioning board? Can we legally accept donations and enter agreements?Strategic planning facilitation; grant writing support; nonprofit financial and accounting services.
Staffed and resourcedDo we have paid staff or a dedicated contractor? A strategic plan and a research budget line?Research operations consulting; advice on hiring a research lead; introductions to research funders.
SustainableDo we have diversified funding and someone leading research? Could the organization survive its founder's departure?Executive coaching and succession planning; reserve building; governance maturity review.

Scientific and community engagement

StageQuestions to ask yourselvesWhat tends to help
Peer supportDo members share experiences and information?Education on how patient experience informs research; an introduction to patient reported data.
Awareness and connectingDo we help families find clinicians or trials? Do we raise awareness beyond our members?Clinician and researcher landscape mapping; trial finder navigation.
Priorities from communityHave we asked our community what research is important to them? Is there a group that channels that into priorities?Facilitation of a priority setting exercise; survey design.
Advisory boardDo we have a scientific or medical advisory board that meets and influences decisions?Help recruiting credible experts; charters and conflict of interest policy.
Agenda settingDo we help define the research agenda for our condition? Have researchers co-designed studies with us?Partnership as a co-investigator; patient engagement written into grant proposals as a named partner.

Ecosystem integration

StageQuestions to ask yourselvesWhat tends to help
IsolatedAre we essentially working on our own?Introductions to consortia and umbrella organizations; an ecosystem map.
Informal tiesDo we have a relationship with at least one researcher or clinician?Warm introductions to relevant consortia such as REN, COMBINEDBrain, and NORD.
Consortium memberAre we an active member of a consortium or umbrella?Advice on participating actively; identifying collaborations within the consortium.
Active collaborationsAre we in shared projects with other organizations?Project management support; multi-site agreement and data governance templates.
Recognized nodeDo others come to us as a partner or convener?Infrastructure or staffing to support convening; co-hosted workshops.

Data infrastructure

StageQuestions to ask yourselvesWhat tends to help
No structured dataIs most of what we know anecdotal or scattered?A plain language primer on registries; help defining what data would be useful to collect.
Contact registryDo we have a maintained list of who our patients are and how to reach them?Advice on consent for re-contact; help selecting a registry platform if that is the direction.
Patient reported registryDo we collect structured patient reported data on a real platform, with meaningful enrollment?Instrument design and validation; recruitment strategy; biostatistics consultation.
Natural history and longitudinalAre we collecting data over time with a defined data model?Natural history study design; clinical data management; REDCap or equivalent setup.
Harmonized to standardsIs our data mapped to common standards and combinable with other datasets?Data harmonization; ontology and common data element mapping; informatics support.

Data governance and sharing

The north star of the model.

StageQuestions to ask yourselvesWhat tends to help
No governanceAre we holding data without a clear consent or privacy framework?Research consent and privacy policies; IRB and ethics navigation.
Internal governanceDo we have consent and privacy policies, with data held internally?Review of whether consent permits future sharing; an introduction to data sharing concepts.
Documented and standardizedDo we have a published data dictionary and standardized variables? Could an outsider understand our dataset from the documentation alone?Building the data dictionary; mapping variables to common data elements; documentation support.
Shared with a partnerHave we shared data with a vetted partner under a formal agreement? Do we have a governance policy for how sharing decisions get made?Data sharing and use agreements; setting up a data access committee; brokering partner relationships.
Open and broadly accessibleIs our data or metadata findable by researchers beyond named partners, meeting FAIR principles? Can qualified researchers discover and request it through a defined process?FAIR-ification; deposit into recognized repositories; publishing discoverable metadata even when raw data stays controlled.

Organizations self-assess against this model; whoswho records the stage an organization reports, and shows nothing where none has been reported. To record a stage for your organization, or to comment on the model, email danielle@boycedatascience.com.
